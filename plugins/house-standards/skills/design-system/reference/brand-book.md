Lilac & Sage is the shared look for every app we build: calm pastel lilac and sage on a lilac-tinted neutral, one friendly sans, soft radii, borders over shadows. Every app uses these tokens and the `ls-` component classes; no app defines its own colours.

## Using this system in an app

- Load `tokens.css` (generated from `tokens.json`) then `components/bundle.css`, once, at the app root. Set `data-theme="light"` or `data-theme="dark"` on `<html>`; follow the OS with `matchMedia('(prefers-color-scheme: dark)')` unless the user has picked a theme on that device with the Appearance setting (Match device, Light, Dark; see `components.md`). Set the attribute in an inline script in `<head>` before first paint so dark-mode users never see a light flash.
- Components reference **semantic tokens only** (`bg`, `surface`, `ink`, `accent`, `sage`, `success-ink`…). The `lilac-*`, `sage-*` and `mist-*` scales are primitives: use them only to define new semantic tokens here, never in app code.
- Publish both files as an internal package (e.g. `@jameswilson1996/ui-tokens`) so every app pins a version; changes land here first, then apps bump.
- Tailwind (optional): map semantic tokens in `@theme`, e.g. `--color-accent: var(--accent); --color-ink: var(--ink);`, so `bg-accent text-on-accent` stays theme-aware. Never use Tailwind's default palette.

## Colour

- **Lilac is the primary brand colour.** `accent` fills the one primary action per view, selected tabs and focus; text on it is always `on-accent` (white in light, deep lilac in dark — never hard-code white).
- **Sage is the secondary.** `sage` with `on-sage` for secondary buttons and positive highlights. Do not use sage for destructive or error states.
- **Pastel fills carry most of the colour.** `accent-soft` for selected rows, nav highlight and soft buttons; status uses the four soft/ink pairs: `success-*` (sage), `warning-*` (peach), `danger-*` (rose), `info-*` (sky).
- **Charts** use `chart-1` to `chart-5` in that fixed order, with `chart-grid`, `chart-axis` and `chart-label`; never the status or fill tokens. A legend or direct labels name every series, and a dashed line marks what is projected.
- **Status is never colour alone.** Every badge or alert says its state in words and/or a Lucide icon; success and danger are also separated by icon shape.
- Text: `ink` for primary, `ink-muted` for secondary. Both reach at least 5.5:1 on `bg`, `surface` and `surface-sunken` in both themes; every ink/fill pair in this system is AA (4.5:1) or better.
- **Dark mode keeps the pastel character.** Solid fills (`accent`, `sage`, `danger`) turn into lighter pastels with deep text on them (`on-accent`, `on-sage`, `on-danger`); soft fills and surfaces turn into deep tinted greys. Never invert colours or pick dark-theme values by hand in an app; every semantic token already has both.
- Borders: `line` for decorative hairlines, `line-strong` for anything a user operates (inputs, checkboxes) — 3:1 in both themes.
- Disabled: each button variant has a fill and text pair (`disabled-fill`/`on-disabled`, `accent-disabled`/`on-accent-disabled`, `sage-disabled`/`on-sage-disabled`, `danger-disabled`/`on-danger-disabled`). Disabled text reaches at least 3.6:1 on its fill in both themes, and a disabled primary stays lilac. Never fade a control with `opacity`.

## Type

- One family: **Figtree** (Google Fonts, weights 400–700) for all UI; **JetBrains Mono** for IDs, codes and keys.
- Scale: `display` 40 · `h1` 30 · `h2` 22 · `h3` 17 · `body` 15 · `body-sm` 13 · `label` 13/600 · `caption` 12 · `code` 13.
- Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings. Use tabular numerals (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) for money and counts. Money inputs are `ls-money` and amounts in text `ls-amount`, formatted with `Intl.NumberFormat`.

## Space, shape, depth

- 4px base: `space-1` 4 → `space-8` 64. Card padding `space-4`; gap between cards `space-5`; between page sections `space-7`.
- Forms (`ls-form`): `form-help-gap` 6 from label to control and control to help text; `form-field-gap` 16 from field to field; `form-actions-gap` 24 from the last field to the actions; `form-section-gap` 32 between sub-sections in a card.
- Radius: `radius-sm` 6 (badges, checkboxes), `radius-md` 10 (buttons, inputs, tabs), `radius-lg` 16 (cards, alerts, dialogs), `radius-pill` (switches, avatars).
- Depth: borders first. `shadow-sm` only to lift a card; `shadow-md` for menus and popovers; `shadow-lg` for dialogs, over a `backdrop` scrim.
- Page: the sidebar is `nav-width` 220, then the page, left-aligned and capped at `content-width` 1040 (boards and wide tables may go full width). Gutters are `space-6` 32 beside the page and `page-gutter` 16 on a phone, plus the safe area. See App shell in `components.md`.
- Overlays: dialogs are `dialog-sm` 400, `dialog-md` 560 or `dialog-lg` 800 wide and fill the screen at 640px and below, except a sheet (`ls-dialog--sheet`): it rises from the bottom as tall as its content, up to 90% of the screen, for confirms, pickers, filters and forms of up to 4 fields. Longer forms stay full screen. Popovers grow to `popover-width` 320 (tooltips `tooltip-width` 240), and sit `popover-offset` 8 from their trigger and the viewport edge. Dialogs keep `overlay-margin` 16 from the viewport edge.
- Controls are 36px tall (28px small) so buttons, inputs and selects align on one line.
- Touch: at 640px and below and on touch screens, every target is at least 44px. Buttons, inputs, selects and money fields are 44px tall, small buttons keep a 44px hit area, checkbox and switch rows are 44px, and field text is 16px so iOS doesn't zoom the page on focus. The bundle does this; apps don't set their own control heights.
- Hover styles are for pointers only: the bundle puts them under `@media (hover: hover)`, so a tap on a phone never leaves a hover fill behind. App CSS does the same.
- Forced colours (Windows High Contrast, #55): the browser replaces every colour with the user's palette and drops fills and shadows. The bundle redraws each state that relied on a fill with system colours: chosen and current items in `SelectedItem`, the selected row, today and drop targets with a `Highlight` ring, switch, radio and progress marks, placeholders in `GrayText`, and badges and chips outlined. A new component that shows a state with a fill or shadow alone adds its own rule to that block, and is checked with Playwright's `emulateMedia({ forcedColors: "active" })` in a dark and a light palette.
- Focus: a 2px solid `focus-ring` outline offset 2px on every interactive element (`:focus-visible`). Never remove it.

## Motion

Motion shows what you are holding, what changed, what arrived, where you went, or what a link points at. It never decorates.

- Durations come only from tokens: `motion-quick` 120ms (colour, press states, chevrons, the old page when switching tabs, anything leaving), `motion-base` 150ms (dialogs, popovers and toasts arriving, the new page fading in after a tab switch, rows sliding aside, a drop settling, a row folding away), `motion-travel` 200ms (movement across most of the screen: a full-screen dialog or sheet on a phone, a page sliding in when you drill into it). `motion-flash` 1200ms is how long `ls-flash` marks a row. `motion-wait` 400ms is how long something may take before it shows it is working; `motion-pulse` 1600ms is one breath of a placeholder and `motion-spin` 800ms one turn of a busy spinner.
- Easing: `ease-enter` for arriving and moving, `ease-exit` for leaving. Leaving is faster than arriving. No bounce or overshoot.
- Animate only `opacity`, `translate`, `scale`, `rotate` and colour. Never animate layout on large content; a single row may fold.
- Reduced motion: the three durations become 0ms under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, so anything built on the tokens stops moving by itself. A dragged item still follows the pointer, because the user is moving it. `ls-flash` keeps its mark for 1.2s and clears it without fading. Placeholders stop pulsing, and a busy spinner turns three times slower, since a still one reads as stuck.
- The six patterns:
  1. Holding (drag): the item lifts in `motion-quick` (`ls-lifted`: shadow, accent border, 2% larger; board cards also tilt 1.5° with `ls-lifted--tilt`) and then follows the pointer exactly, with no easing. A dashed `ls-drop-slot` opens where it will land and the items around it slide aside in `motion-base`. On release it settles into the slot in `motion-base`. Rows drag by a handle at once; cards with no handle lift after a 350ms press, with a 15ms vibration where supported, so a swipe still scrolls. Arrow keys on the handle move it one place with the same slide. Holding an item near the edge of a scrolling board scrolls it, so every column can be reached on any screen, and holding it near the top or bottom of a list or column that runs past the screen scrolls that way, so every place in it can be reached. The top and bottom are where the content shows, inside the tab bar and a stuck tab row, never under them.
  2. Changing a list: a ticked item is marked at once, then folds away after 400ms. A new item fades in at its place (`ls-enter`). Amounts change in place with no counting up. A toast with Undo confirms what changed.
     A row that moves to another place in its list after a command (a bill marked paid whose next due date sorts it lower; decided 12 November, #100) slides there over `motion-base` while the rows between slide aside, as in a drag. If it lands outside the screen it is marked with `ls-flash`, the page doesn't scroll to it, and the toast says where it went ("Council tax paid. Next due 1 Dec."). A reorder from another device moves each row the same way. A whole list re-sorting (a column header, a filter) never slides: rows would fly past each other, so it changes at once, or fades its tab panel when it is a navigation. A row that moves between two lists on the page (To get and Picked up) leaves one as a ticked row does and fades in at its place in the other (`ls-enter`).
  3. Arriving and leaving: dialogs, menus, detail cards and the filter panel rise 8px and fade in over `motion-base`; on a phone a full-screen dialog or a sheet rises from the bottom in `motion-travel` while the backdrop fades. Tooltips fade in over `motion-quick`, since they follow the pointer. All of them leave the reverse way and faster: they fade and drop 8px over `motion-quick`, and on a phone a full-screen dialog or sheet slides off the bottom edge over `motion-base`. Everything that arrives with motion leaves with motion, including an item that faded in (`ls-leave`); nothing that animated in vanishes at once. A closing layer never blocks the next tap. Toasts rise from the bottom and leave in `motion-quick`. Hover colour changes fade over `motion-quick` on every control.
  4. Navigation: only the page moves (decided 9 November). The shell around it (the sidebar with the app's name, places and account, the tab bar and toasts) never moves when the page changes, and the current place in it changes at once. Switching between top-level places (sidebar or bottom tab bar) fades through (decided 9 November, replacing the crossfade, which showed two pages at once): the old page fades out over `motion-quick`, then the new one fades in and grows from 96% over `motion-base`, with no sideways movement. Within a place (a page's tabs, the board's column tabs, another period) the header and tabs hold still and only the content below them fades out over `motion-quick` and in over `motion-base`, with no scale. Opening something one level down slides the new page over the old one from the right in `motion-travel`, and Back slides it off to the right in `motion-base`, the same at every width: with the sidebar still, the page slides beside it on a wide screen just as it fills the screen on a phone. The motion follows where you land, not where the link sits: a link from one place to another (Today's "All bills", a shopping item's meal) fades through like the tab bar, whatever page it lands on, and Back from there fades through too. It never slides, since a slide promises that Back returns to the page underneath.
     While the next page's data loads (decided 12 November, #99): a navigation holds the current page until the next page's main query answers, for up to `motion-wait`, so the motion lands on the finished page. The tapped link or tab shows it was pressed at once, and the current place in the shell changes at once. Past `motion-wait` the motion runs to the page with its placeholders where the content will be, and the content then replaces them in place with no second motion. A change of period or filter on the same page follows the same rule for its tab panel: it never fades to the old figures and then swaps in the new ones. Preloading on hover, focus and touch start makes the wait rare. A page never arrives twice.
     Across the shell's edge (#98): signing out, or a session ending, inside the app fades through to the signed-out page like a change of place; the shell goes at once with the old page. Arriving from the sign-in provider is a fresh page load with no motion.
  5. Pointing: a link to one row scrolls to it smoothly and marks it with `ls-flash`. A link to a record in another place fades through to the page that lists it, then points at its row.
  6. Waiting: nothing for `motion-wait`, then placeholders where the content will be (`ls-loading`), pulsing slowly, or a spinner before a busy button's label. The button keeps its width. For a page change, the wait happens on the current page (pattern 4), so the placeholders arrive with the page's own motion rather than after it.
- Never: page-load sequences or staggered entrances, sliding between tabs, numbers counting up, parallax, blur, zoom-in screens (fade through's 96% is the only scale on a screen), skeleton shimmer, a spinner for anything under 400ms, celebrations, or motion that blocks the next tap. Every transition can be interrupted.

## Voice

- Plain, warm, brief. Address the user as "you"; the product speaks as "we" only in empty states and errors.
- Buttons are verbs: "Save changes", "Invite member". Errors say what happened and what to do: "Sync failed. The billing service didn't respond. Try again in a minute."
- Errors name the thing and what didn't happen, then the next step: "Bills didn't load. Try again in a minute.", "That didn't save.", "Couldn't mark Council tax paid". Never "Something went wrong", "Oops" or an error code alone. Who changed something is named: "Sam changed this bill."
- No emoji in UI, no exclamation marks, no ALL CAPS.
- State facts. Empty states (`ls-empty`), tooltips and onboarding say what something is or does: "Projects you create appear here." Skip contrast framing ("Not just a list, a workspace"), marketing adjectives (seamless, powerful, effortless) and rhetorical questions.
- Use real numbers and names: "3 of 10 seats used", "Last synced 2 minutes ago".

## Iconography

- **Lucide** icons (outline, 2px stroke, round caps), drawn with `currentColor` so they take the text colour. 18px in nav, 20px in the tab bar and alerts, 16px inside buttons.
- **The mark** (decided 12 November, #97): two leaves from one stem, lilac on the left and sage on the right and taller, in `mark-lilac` and `mark-sage`. Those two tokens are for the mark only; they reach 3:1 on `bg`, `surface` and `accent-soft` in both themes. Files: `assets/Logos/mark.svg` and `mark-dark.svg`; the path data is `MARK_LEAVES` in `@jameswilson1996/ui-tokens/icons.js`.
- Never recolour, outline, rotate, stretch or add effects to the mark, and never put it on `accent` or a photo. In forced colours it is drawn in `CanvasText`.
- Clear space: a quarter of the mark's height on every side, kept free of other content. Smallest size: 16px (a favicon); 24px beside the app's name.
- With the app's name (`ls-brand`): the mark at 24px, `space-2`, then the name in Figtree 600, 17/24, `ink`, -0.01em tracking, sentence case as the app writes it. Larger, on signed-out pages (`ls-brand--lg`): 40px and 22/28 with `space-3`. The name is never set in the mark's colours.
- Where it shows: the sidebar's head, the signed-out pages, the favicon and the installed app's icons. On a phone the sidebar is hidden, so pages lead with their own title; an app that keeps a top row on a phone leads it with `ls-brand`.
- App icons: the mark on an `accent-soft` tile, from `appIcons()` in `icons.js` (see The head in `components.md`). An app that sits beside other house apps on one home screen passes its own Lucide glyph, drawn in `on-accent-soft` on the same tile.

## Data & state in the UI

- The backend owns state and actions. The UI renders server state (TanStack Query) and sends commands; it never infers a status the API didn't send. Badges map 1:1 to the API's state enum.
- Validation errors come from the API (problem+json) and appear in the matching field's help line with `data-invalid`.
- Details and filtered lists open in an `ls-dialog` over the current page, with the open state in the URL, so Back or Escape returns the user to where they were.
